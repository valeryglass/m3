# Language and Safety Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines safe language for describing MISHA to users, professionals,
partners, investors, and marketing/SMM audiences.

MISHA should be described as a self-observation and reflection system. It
should not be described as therapy, diagnosis, treatment, emergency support, or
professional replacement.

## Good terms

- self-observation tool
- structured episode journal
- personal pattern map
- gentle mirror
- recurring patterns
- self-understanding
- reflection system
- helps notice links
- helps prepare material for a professional

## Bad / prohibited terms

- AI therapist
- AI psychologist
- therapy replacement
- diagnostic tool
- treats anxiety
- fixes thinking
- detects disorders
- knows you better than you know yourself
- personality detector
- mental health diagnosis

## Safe rewrite examples

Bad:

> You have an avoidance pattern caused by anxiety.

Good:

> In several episodes, tension appears close to postponing or leaving the situation. This may be an important recurring link to observe.

Bad:

> You are afraid of judgment.

Good:

> In the described episodes, evaluation and mistakes often appear near tension.

Bad:

> You self-sabotage.

Good:

> Some episodes show a repeated gap between intention and action.

## Principles

- Describe observations, not identity.
- Use uncertainty when needed.
- Do not diagnose.
- Do not prescribe treatment.
- Do not claim hidden motives.
- Do not shame the user.
- Do not present generated reports as clinical truth.
- Keep the user's agency intact.

## Practical language rules

Say "the described episodes show" instead of "you are."

Say "may be a recurring link" instead of "this proves."

Say "worth observing" instead of "must be fixed."

Say "grounded in user-provided material" or "based on described episodes"
instead of medical-sounding claims of efficacy.

Keep the mirror, map, and fork metaphors modest. A mirror reflects. A map
connects. A fork marks a possible difference. None of these metaphors should be
used to imply diagnosis, authority, or certainty.
